*Originally published on Medium in October 2023, while Prosper was still running. Cut down, with the ending added in October 2026.*

In 2020 I ran a clothing brand while I was still in school. We learned fast that digital ads rarely paid for themselves. Ad costs had been climbing for years, and most small brands spend on them anyway because there's no obvious alternative. So we turned to referrals.

Every referral tool we looked at seemed built ten years earlier. Ugly UI, worse UX. Each one lived in a pop-up, and each one asked the customer to create an account, download an app, scan a QR code, or worst of all, wait for a friend to buy before getting anything.

Word of mouth worked better than ads. There was just no easy way for a company to run it or track it. So my co-founder and I spent close to two years building Prosper: a referral widget that took twenty seconds and lived on the product page, and the platform a store needed to run it without us.

## Two years in one paragraph

The MVP shipped in October 2022 with more features than an MVP needed and friction we underestimated. We added unique discount codes per customer so stores could trust the numbers. We built a brand and a website, three times. We built a dashboard so stores could set up their own campaigns and design their own widgets. We built a Shopify app so Prosper could reach millions of stores and show up after checkout, not just before. Then I redesigned the widget from everything users had told us about the first one. [The product](/work/prosper-product) and [the brand](/work/prosper-brand) each have their own page with the screens.

That's where the original post ended. "Every product has a period of fast-paced innovation followed by a period of slow optimization. I'm ready to innovate again."

## The ending

Prosper stopped operating in 2024 and was dissolved in 2025.

The product worked. Our best pilot client made about $6,300 in a month from referrals and said Prosper did more in 30 days than his old tools had in three years.

Then we went to raise. Months of due diligence with several investors, and in 2024 the only thing anyone wanted to fund was AI. The raise didn't close, and we ran out of money and energy at the same time.

That's the true story, and for a long time it was the only story I told. Here's what I'd do differently.

**Charge for the value, not the event.** Our pricing was $25 a month plus 75 cents per referral. That client made $6,300 in a month and paid us about $72. We designed a product that created real value and captured almost none of it, and we called that "risk-free pricing." I'd put a real price on it from the first customer. Ten customers at $500 a month would have told us more than 86 at $25.

**Decide early what kind of company it is.** At that price Prosper was a tool business. We raised like a venture business. Either could have worked: a small, profitable tool that didn't need anyone's permission, or a venture company priced to grow into its round. Doing both at once is what killed it, more than the market did.

Design is verified by demand. The widget passed that test. The business model around it didn't, and I'm the one who designed both.
