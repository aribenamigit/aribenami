## Context

Modernistic is a print manufacturer in Stillwater, Minnesota, founded in 1938, with a Fortune 500 client base. In 2021 the company was partway through a rebrand: an outside agency had set the new identity (logo, type, color) and nobody inside the building owned what came next. I was hired as the first in-house graphic designer to apply the identity to everything the company put in front of customers.

## Role

Everything after the logo. I built the templates, the print system, and the voice of the applied brand, and I illustrated. I also learned print production from the inside, on the floor of a company that prints for a living.

## What I built

**Sell sheets.** A two-page system for the product catalog: a full-bleed photo cover with the product name as the headline, and a back page of highlights, applications and facts. Shown here for custom anti-slip graphics and the sustainable magnetic receptive line.

**Case studies.** A matching system for customer stories, in light and dark versions. The paint-masking study for a supercar manufacturer is the one I'd show: masks that cut masking time to a third and waste by 65%.

**2022 Sustainability Report.** A ten-page report covering the company's environmental strategy, water usage, past initiatives and goals, with the charts built from their numbers.

**Holiday packaging.** A mailer box for customers, designed on the dieline with an icon system drawn from the company's own equipment and services, printed in one color on kraft.

**Candle label and greeting card.** Small pieces with copy that does the work: "yes, we are a printer. yes, we made this candle. no, there isn't a project we can't handle."

**Visitor sign-in screen.** The brand's angled bars as a screen graphic for the lobby iPad.

**Product mockups.** A printer sells things that don't exist yet, so a lot of the job was photorealism: building the mockup of a product before it was printed. The hotel bedroom below is a composite I made to show a custom wallpaper for a large Las Vegas hotel; the wallpaper in the photo isn't real. Same for most of the product imagery on the sell sheets.

**Sales flyers.** One-page pieces for the sales team, like the digital-plus-screen-printing flyer below.

**The mural.** A line illustration of the company and the town: the Stillwater lift bridge, the presses, the plotter, the delivery truck, the school bus, the CMYK bottles, "Est. 1938." Drawn for a wall and then reused on the holiday card background and on merchandise.

## Outcome

<!-- GAP: anything measurable? Number of pieces produced, catalog coverage, a customer or sales comment, how long the templates stayed in use after you left. If nothing, this section becomes two sentences on what the company had at the end that it didn't have at the start. -->

By the time I left for Prosper, the company had a print system it could run without a designer in the room: templates for sell sheets and case studies, a report format, packaging, and an illustration library it could keep reusing.

## Lesson

An identity is a promise; the applications are where it gets kept. Most rebrands stall between the two because the agency leaves and nobody is paid to do the boring part. The boring part is the brand.
