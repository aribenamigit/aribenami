## Context

A startup's brand has one job: make a new kind of product understandable to someone who has never used one. Prosper was a referral widget, not a category people searched for. The identity, the website, the deck and the whitepaper all had to explain the same thing in a few seconds, to a Shopify store owner, a marketing director, and an investor.

## Role

All of it, from the logo to the last LinkedIn post. The website I designed and built myself, three times. The product itself is [its own case study](/work/prosper-product).

## What I built

**Identity.** The company started as "ReferralOS" with a geometric mark and ended as "prosper" with a rounded wordmark and a P symbol in bright green on dark green. The color and type explorations in between are below, including a purple direction we didn't take.

**Website, three versions.** V1 in 2022 was an illustrated landing page explaining the four features. V2 moved to product screenshots. V3 in 2023 went dark, with the widget components floating over a referral chart as the hero and 3D renders I built in Blender and Adobe Dimension for the feature sections: an envelope on a monitor, a stack of coins, a storefront with the P symbol.

**The whitepaper and the decks.** A seven-page whitepaper in late 2022 for the first fundraise. An investor deck in June 2022 under the ReferralOS name, and the January 2024 deck for the pre-seed round, the one I'm proudest of as a layout: a warm off-white, highlighter yellow and green, product mockups on every product slide, the beta numbers on one slide.

**Client materials.** A case study system (the B Draddy one is the finished example), a product specs document for each integration (Carico Coffee's is below: design overview, suggested placements, incentives, marketing language), printed in-store signage and an email campaign for Home Means Nevada.

**Social.** The LinkedIn feed: the Shopify App Store launch, a year in review, the B Draddy quote, SMS referrals, holidays. Same palette, same type, every post.

## Outcome

The brand survived three website rebuilds, two decks, and a product that changed under it. The January 2024 deck got the company into months of due diligence. That's not the ending I wanted, but it's what a deck is for.

## Lesson

I rebuilt the website three times in two years because the product kept changing and I couldn't stand the old one. Twice was probably right. The third time was design for its own sake.
